What Is Balinese Jewelry?

Balinese jewelry refers to ornamental pieces — rings, earrings, pendants, bracelets and more — produced by craftspeople on the island of Bali, Indonesia, using techniques and aesthetic principles rooted in the island's Hindu-Dharma spiritual culture. While "Bali jewelry" is sometimes used loosely to describe any jewelry with a tropical or bohemian feel, authentic Balinese jewelry is defined by its origin and its craft: pieces made by hand, in Bali, by artisans trained in the island's silver and goldsmithing lineage.

The tradition traces its formal origins to the royal courts of the 14th–16th centuries, when Javanese Hindu culture profoundly influenced Bali following the fall of the Majapahit empire. Metalworking guilds were established under royal patronage, and the craft was passed from master to apprentice within family workshops — a system largely still in place today. Silver, revered for its purity and its association with the moon, became the dominant material, leading to the creation of the elaborate sterling silver work for which Bali is now internationally famous.

The village of Celuk, located south of Bali, is historically considered the silversmithing capital of Bali, with virtually every household engaged in the craft at some level. Bali itself, Bali's cultural and artistic hub, is home to ateliers producing some of the island's finest contemporary Balinese jewelry — including AMBERRA's workshop, where traditional techniques meet the rare beauty of Baltic amber.

Traditional Balinese Silversmithing Techniques

What separates authentic Balinese jewelry from mass-produced imitations is the depth of its technique. our silversmiths employ a range of methods that require years — often a decade or more — to master. The most celebrated include:

  • Granulation: Tiny spheres of silver are fused to a base metal surface without solder, using a copper-salt bonding technique. The result is a textured, dimensional surface of extraordinary intricacy.
  • Filigree: Fine silver wire is twisted, coiled and soldered into lace-like openwork patterns. True Balinese filigree is always hand-formed, never cast or stamped.
  • Repoussé and chasing: Sheet silver is worked from behind (repoussé) and refined from the front (chasing) to create three-dimensional relief forms — floral motifs, mythological figures and sacred geometry being most common.
  • Stone setting: our silversmiths are skilled bezel and prong setters, securing stones — traditionally local gems, and in AMBERRA's case, Baltic amber — in hand-formed mounts that allow light to interact with the stone at maximum depth.

Baltic Amber in Bali Jewelry

Baltic amber is the fossilised resin of ancient trees that grew along the shores of the Baltic Sea some 44 million years ago. Prized since the Neolithic period, it is one of the world's oldest traded luxury materials — ancient Roman merchants called it "the gold of the north." Its warm honey tones range from pale champagne to deep cognac and rare green, each piece unique in its inclusions, clarity and color depth.

How to Identify Authentic Balinese Jewelry

The global popularity of Bali jewelry has unfortunately given rise to a significant volume of machine-made imitations — items marketed as "Balinese style" but manufactured in industrial facilities with no connection to the island's craft tradition. Knowing what to look for protects your investment and your trust.

Genuine Balinese sterling silver jewelry will carry a 925 hallmark, indicating 92.5% pure silver content. Examine the surface carefully: authentic handwork will show slight asymmetries in filigree patterns, variation in granule sizes, and tool marks that reflect individual human touch. Machine-made pieces display a perfection of repetition that, paradoxically, signals inauthenticity. Ask your retailer for the workshop of origin and, where possible, visit or verify the Bali studio directly.
